The kitchen lab

build your own qubit, in hardware — a shopping list and nine bench cards

The tutorial's ball is not a diagram waiting for a quantum computer: everything up to and including step 14 runs on bright, everyday light, and the equipment is sold as toys. This page is the companion for a physical demonstration table. Buy the core kit, cut and mount the film, and every claim the tutorial makes about polarized light — Malus's odds, the twin test, the state that isn't there, light resurrected by an obstacle — becomes a measurement your audience performs themselves. Prices are typical for Amazon / AliExpress / educational suppliers; search terms are given per item.

BUILD NOTE — Cut the polarizing film with scissors, mark each square's transmission axis (rotate it against your phone screen: the darkest orientation is crossed with the screen's axis), and glue each square into a cardboard frame over a printed dial. Mark the QWP film's fast axis the same way: between crossed polarizers, the plate goes darkest when its axis lines up with either one. For metering, fix the lamp–phone distance, dim the room, and read ratios, not absolute lux — the tutorial's percentages are ratios.

PART 2Nine bench cards

Each card names the tutorial step it demonstrates, sketches the optical train, and states the reading the theory predicts — so the audience can check the geometry against the meter.

1The rope and the fence — a wave you can holdSTEP 6

Tie one end of the rope to a doorknob, pass it between the vertical bars of a chair back (or through a cardboard slot), and shake the free end. Shake up-and-down: the wave sails through the vertical slots. Shake sideways: the fence swallows it. Shake at a slant — both wiggles at once — and only the vertical part survives: the fence is a polarizer, and your hand just felt Malus's law. Add a second fence turned 90° after the first: nothing gets through at all — crossed polarizers, in rope. This is step 6's animated wave, at arm's length: the two wiggle sizes are how hard you shake each way.

2Malus's law — odds as brightnessSTEPS 6–7

Lamp → sheet P1 (prepares the beam at β) → sheet P2 (asks the question θ) → phone meter. Take the reading behind P1 alone as 100%, then step P2 through the dial:

θ − β0°30°45°60°90°
meter (cos²)100%75%50%25%0%

Then remove P1 and point the meter at the bare bulb through one rotating sheet: a stubborn, flat ~50% at every angle — the center of the disk, on sale at every supermarket.

3The twin test — 45° is not 135°STEPS 7–10

Prepare two beams: P1 at 45°, then P1 at 135°. Test each with the second sheet, first at 0°, then at 45°:

test sheetbeam at 45° (coin)beam at 135° (anti-coin)
θ = 0° — "the coin flip"50%50%
θ = 45° — the tilted question100%0%

Identical to the flip, opposite to the tilted question — the mirror twins of step 8, told apart in hardware. This ten-second table is the whole reason the half-disk had to grow.

4Glare — the interior of the disk, in the wildSTEP 7

Partially polarized light — the blend slider of step 7 — is all around you. Find a glare: lamplight reflecting off a smooth table, a phone lying face-down, or water. Look at it through one rotating sheet with the lux app behind: the reading breathes, but not to zero. Note the maximum and the minimum; then the degree of polarization is

computeV = (max − min) / (max + min)
V = 1pure beam — the rim
0 < V < 1partially polarized — the interior, at radius V
V = 0the bulb — dead center

You have just measured a seat inside the disk — a confidence score, read off a tabletop. Steeper or shallower reflection angles change V; so does blue sky, 90° away from the sun (try it — skylight is partially polarized, which is how bees navigate). And this, at last, is what polarizing sunglasses are for: road glare is mostly horizontal, so the lenses mount their slots vertically.

5The state that isn't thereSTEP 12

Make circular light: the phone's white screen (a linear source) + QWP with its fast axis at 45° to the screen's axis. Sweep the test sheet: flat ~50% at every θ — the detective kit says "dead center, same as the bulb". Now insert the second QWP (dashed) before the test sheet: full breathing returns, 100%/0% — a pure diagonal beam recovered from "total ignorance", with no light lost. Repeat with the bulb instead of the screen: the plate changes nothing. A blend has no timing to adjust; this beam was never a blend.

Party version: put on RealD 3D glasses and look at yourself in a mirror — one eye goes dark, because reflection flips the corkscrew's handedness (left-circular becomes right-circular).

6Light resurrected — three sheetsSTEP 14

Cross P1 (0°) and P2 (90°): darkness. Slide the third sheet between them at 45° (dashed): light returns. With the reading behind P1 as 100%:

configurationmeter (vs. behind P1)
P1 → P2 crossed0%
P1 → 45° → P225%

An added obstacle produced more light — interference, the sign doing real work, at the kitchen table. Slowly rotate the middle sheet from 0° to 90° and watch the output rise to its 25% maximum at 45° and die at both ends (cos²θ·sin²θ).

7The delay dial, painted — tape artSTEP 13

Layer strips of clear tape at assorted angles on a piece of glass or stiff plastic. Place it between crossed polarizers against the lamp: the invisible sandwich explodes into color. Tape is birefringent — each layer delays one wiggle relative to the other, and the delay depends on wavelength, so each thickness turns the dial φ by a different amount per color. Rotate the back sheet by 90° and every color jumps to its complement. This is the tutorial's dial made visible: a delay you can laminate.

8Measuring is asking — calciteSTEP 9

Set the calcite rhomb on printed text: every letter appears twice — the crystal splits light into two perpendicular polarizations that take different paths. Look through a rotating polarizer: at one angle only the first image survives, a quarter-turn later only the second, in between both with cos²-traded brightness. One object, performing "pick a diameter, answer with an endpoint" — the double image is the question's two answers, physically separated.

9Fringes and the eraser — two pathsSTEP 15

Tape two pencil leads side by side (or use a single hair) and shine the laser through the gap onto a wall a few meters away: bands of light and dark — two routes, adding and cancelling. Then the eraser, with three small film pieces: cover one slit with 0° film and the other with 90° film — the fringes vanish (the routes are now labeled, and labeled routes cannot cancel). Hold a third piece at 45° in the beam after the slits — the fringes return, dimmer. The label is erased; the routes cancel again. This bench, run one photon at a time in a real lab, gives identical curves — step 15's claim.

SAFETY — Use a class-2 laser pointer (<1 mW), never aim it at eyes or reflective surfaces at eye height, and keep it pointed at the wall. Everything else on this page is film, tape, cardboard and lamplight.
HONESTY NOTE — Every card above runs on bright, classical light, and that is exactly the point of steps 6–14: the ball, the odds, the sign and the dial are all measurable classically. What this table cannot show is step 15's genuinely quantum half — single photons, one click at a time. That takes a university lab (a photon-pair source and single-photon detectors, thousands of euros). The honest framing works in your favor: the kitchen bench draws the curves exactly, and quantum mechanics is the discovery that the same curves survive when the light arrives as indivisible clicks.
