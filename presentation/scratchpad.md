# Build your own qubit — deck outline

Audience: non-technical; challenging/teasing tone OK. Visuals reuse tutorial palette:
paper #FFFFFF, grid #E1F3FC/#AFE0F7, ink #002157, inkSoft #5C6E8F, gold #EE7203, teal #00A1E4, red #F71D25.
Fonts: Fraunces (serif) + IBM Plex Mono (labels). Graph-paper diagram cards.

Titles (short declarative sentences):
1. Build your own qubit (title, ink bg)
2. In the classical world, every answer is definite
3. At small scales, answers become probabilities
4. One number hides where the uncertainty comes from
5. A second number separates the two coins  → upper Bernoulli semicircle
6. Mixed beliefs fill the half-disk
7. Every point has a hidden twin  → full circle via orientation
8. The switch becomes a dial, the circle becomes a ball  → Bloch ball
9. A richer data structure is the power of quantum (bit → interval → circle → ball strip)
10. Bonus · What makes it really quantum (divider, ink bg)
11. Measuring is asking "which end?" (diameters, squared-distance rule)
12. Rotating the question is real physics (interferometers; twins split)
13. Measuring is judging how far apart states sit (hemisphere, π/4, cos²)

Type scale: title 64px / divider 88px, body 30px lh1.55, mono labels 24px, kicker 24px.
Padding 90/100. Split layout: left text ~44%, right graph-paper diagram card.
